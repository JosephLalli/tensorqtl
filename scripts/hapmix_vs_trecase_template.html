hapmixQTL vs TReCASE

Both methods combine total expression with allele-specific expression to find cis-eQTLs. hapmixQTL works on Salmon estimates from each donor's personalized transcriptome and weights donors by their measurement uncertainty. TReCASE fits a joint likelihood to read counts. This page puts every comparison between them in one place.

Method status: half-read split adopted

On 29 September 2026, half-read split became the production association default: point-estimate ASE with Gibbs-informed weights, total expression log2((count + 0.5)/(effective library size + 1) × 106), unit total weights, and fitted residual scales. Existing expression PCs remain fixed.

This page preserves the earlier measurements. Here, split uses the original log2(CPM + 1) total phenotype; gibbs is the prior default with Gibbs weights in both channels. These results were not rerun under the new default. See the half-read comparison and implementation checks. Adoption accepts improved beta recovery with a precision tradeoff; it does not establish uniform precision improvement or SuSiE credible-set calibration.

Planted effects: who finds more true genes

Real expression records from this cohort, shuffled against the genotypes so no real eQTL remains, with a known effect planted at one variant in half of 100 genes. TReCASE was run twice: on Salmon estimates, as the other methods are, and on its own alignment counts (featureCounts totals, phASER allele counts, per strand, WASP-filtered). The middle panel gives both methods the same alignment counts, so any gap there is the model, not the input.

Effect size: how close the estimate is to the planted value

Mean estimated effect divided by the planted effect at the causal variant, for a 1.32-fold planted effect. 1.0 is unbiased. Genes are grouped by their read depth.

hapmixQTL's shortfall is concentrated in genes under 100 reads; the same shortfall on alignment counts (table) places it in the estimator, not in Salmon. Most of it is the total channel's log2(CPM + 1), whose 1-CPM pseudocount is about 17 reads here; the rest is the allelic channel's Gibbs-variance weights, computed from the same counts as the allelic ratio (effect-size shortfall). On TReCASE's own model

A simulation that generates data exactly as TReCASE assumes: negative-binomial totals and beta-binomial allele counts, 500 datasets per setting. hapmixQTL does not assume this model. Power is matched: each method is held to its own 95th percentile under no effect, so a miscalibrated method cannot win on false positives.

"TReCASE likelihood" is the generating model's own joint likelihood test. "asSeq, as run" is the published TReCASE software's reported p, which switches to its total-count test when its two channels disagree. Standard errors of matched power are 0.01 to 0.04 (table).

Real data: replication in 135 held-out donors

Each method maps the real 92 donors; its top K genes are checked in 135 other donors (same direction, p < 0.05). TReCASE ran on a seeded random 1,500 genes, so every method is restricted to those. Below zero, hapmixQTL's top genes replicate more often than TReCASE's.
