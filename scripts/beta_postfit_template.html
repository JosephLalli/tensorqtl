Can a post-fit adjustment recover the planted effect?

Why this was asked

What was run

Total rescale
Divide the total slope and its standard error by ĉ, the share of a fold change that log2(count + 0.5) passes, averaged over donors with the regression's own genotype leverage (ĉ = Σ x̃ᵢcᵢxᵢ / Σ x̃ᵢxᵢ, cᵢ = countᵢ/(countᵢ + 0.5), x̃ the genotype after the covariates are regressed out).
Reweight
Refit the allelic slope with each donor's Gibbs variance evaluated at the allele fraction the fit predicts rather than the observed one, iterated to a fixed point. Near balance this is the full-balance weighting rejected on 2026-09-29.
Binomial bootstrap
Keep the shipped Gibbs weights; simulate each gene's allele counts from the fitted model with binomial noise, refit the shipped slope on each of 200 replicates, and find the effect whose average simulated slope equals the observed one (a bias correction by inverting the mean of the estimator).
Wild bootstrap
The same inversion, but each replicate keeps every donor's own residual with a random sign (swapping the haplotype labels is a symmetry of the null), so the simulated spread is the data's own, overdispersion included.
Pooled wild factor
One attenuation factor per gene set: the median over its genes of observed ÷ wild-corrected slope, over both effect sizes so it never sees the planted value, applied to every allelic slope and standard error.
MSE ratio
Mean squared error of slope/planted − 1 under the adjustment, divided by the same for the shipped slope; below 1 is better. Whiskers and brackets are 95% gene-clustered intervals (genes resampled 2,000 times with all their units).
